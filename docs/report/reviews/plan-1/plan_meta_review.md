## Bottom line

Proceed **only with a reduced, validated study**, not the 16,416-call sweep. The most important change is to replace the headline “correlated reviewer errors” claim with a test of **shared case-level blind spots and failure of the pooled-iid reliability approximation**: the current design cannot distinguish conditional reviewer dependence from differences in case difficulty. Make honest error and static committee aggregation the core; leave persuasion optional and keep enforcement claims tied to the kernel paths actually tested.

## Disagreements you resolved (and how)

- **Large staged sweep versus a genuinely smaller study:** Adopt A’s approximately 800-call core. B and D’s staging improvements help operationally but do not resolve the task-count limitation or the workload of the full sweep. C’s additional cases are valuable only if they can be validated without delaying the core; do not author 12–20 extra cases under deadline pressure.

- **What committee resampling establishes:** Accept everyone’s recommendation to remove single-draw luck, but reject the stronger suggestion that resampling alone makes RQ2 adequately powered or establishes dependence. It estimates aggregation behavior from a finite response pool; uncertainty across the small set of PRs remains.

- **Which independence baseline is valid:** Keep pooled-iid failure as an informative practical comparison, not a dependence test. Add a separately calibrated case-specific baseline. A within-case permutation that fixes observed error counts is not independent evidence of dependence, and a simple cross-model product is not the correct failure probability for a general threshold committee. Use the full threshold calculation.

- **Persuasion controls:** A fixed-board randomized experiment is preferable to salvaging live waves. A 0%-colluder difference-in-differences and exposure logs would improve a retained live analysis, but neither difference-in-differences nor wave trends isolate argument content when board histories and tallies change endogenously. Label such results as total board-exposure effects.

- **Label validation and difficulty tuning:** Require policy-grounded fixtures and adjudication, not model consensus. Resolve ambiguities before inference where possible; retain any unresolved, preidentified disputes in an appendix and sensitivity analysis. Do not exclude cases based on observed reviewer performance or silently rewrite them after seeing results.

- **Forged receipts and optional baselines:** Remove forged receipts from semantic reviewer-error denominators and paid reviewer experiments. Keep cheap deterministic kernel regression tests separately. The static-analysis baseline suggested by D is useful only if an existing tool runs immediately; custom rule-writing is lower priority than valid labels and uncertainty.

## What to cut if time runs short

1. **All live waves and RQ5**, including optional fixed-board exposure.
2. **The eight-regime catalogue:** retain a small predeclared set of threshold rules; drop hybrid and redundant variants first.
3. External model panels, custom static-analysis baselines, additional cases, and difficulty-tier significance claims.
4. Extra figures, demos and broad agent-economy framing.
5. If necessary, reduce the validated case set and present a pilot—**do not preserve scope by dropping label checks, operational-failure reporting or case-level uncertainty**.

Never cut the limitations/dual-use appendix, raw denominators, or final submission checks.
